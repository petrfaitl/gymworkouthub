4 × 6

📝 Explanations & Strategy

🎯 Why these exercises?

  • Swim: Lat pulldown, rows, and overhead press build pulling strength and shoulder stability.
  • Bike: Hip thrusts, RDLs, and KB swings develop glute/hamstring power for the downstroke and aero position.
  • Run: Bulgarian split squats, single‑leg work, and calf raises improve running economy and balance.
  • Overall durability: Core work (planks, Pallof press) and grip (farmer's carries) keep you stable when fatigued.

🗓️ Combining with swim/bike/run

  • Tuesday (Gym + Bike): Gym session first, then an easy 20–30 min spin (Zone 2). No hard intervals.
  • Thursday (Gym + Bike): Same pattern – gym priority, then light bike. Avoid intense efforts after leg day.
  • Monday/Friday (Swim only): Use these days for low‑impact recovery.
  • Weekend (Long ride / run): Keep Thursday as your last gym day – at least 48 h before long efforts.

📏 Progression & Weight Selection

  • 4×6 rep range: Aim for 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR 1–2) on the last set. Increase weight when you can comfortably complete all sets with good form.
  • Suggested starting targets: Goblet squat 32 kg, hip thrust +55 kg, RDL 40 kg, lat pulldown 75 kg, overhead press 16 kg.
  • Increments: Add 2.5–5 kg for upper body, 5–10 kg for lower body when ready.

🚫 Common pitfalls

  • Too much gym cardio: The rower/elliptical is only for warm‑ups – don't replace sport sessions.
  • Excessive soreness: If you're too stiff to swim/run well, dial back the load or volume.
  • Skipping core: Always include at least 2 core exercises per gym day – it saves energy in all three sports.
  • Training to failure: Stop 1–2 reps short of failure to avoid spiking fatigue and compromising outdoor sessions.

💡 Final tip

This gym work is supplemental. Your race performance comes from swim/bike/run volume. Use this plan to build a robust foundation. As race season approaches, reduce to 1 maintenance session per week (2 × 6–8 reps at ~80% weight) to keep strength without fatigue.
