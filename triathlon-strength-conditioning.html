📌 Rep Range Philosophy – Heavy vs. Light for Endurance Athletes

Compound lifts (squat, deadlift, hip thrust): Heavier and lower‑reps (4–8) builds maximum strength and neuromuscular power with minimal added muscle mass – added mass is pure cost for an endurance athlete.

Unilateral & stability work: Keep at moderate‑to‑high reps (10–15) – these are about motor control and tendon resilience.

🔄 Practical progression through your plan:
  • Base phase (weeks 1–4, Sessions A/B): Moderate reps 10–15, lighter loads – conditions tendons and grooves movement patterns.
  • Build / Peak phases (weeks 6, 9, 11 – rotate in C/D): Heavier loads, lower reps (4–8) on big lifts.
  • Recovery & Taper (weeks 4, 8, 12, 13–16): Drop back to light loads and movement maintenance only.
⚠️ Injury‑history notes (plantar fasciitis & calf strains):
  • Plantar fasciitis (since 2025): Keep the eccentric heel drop in every single gym session.
  • Calf strains: These have historically flared when speed work sits too close to running sessions.
  • During recovery weeks, strength load drops to mobility/activation only.
