📌 Rep Range Philosophy – Heavy vs. Light for Endurance Athletes

Compound lifts (squat, deadlift, hip thrust): Heavier and lower‑reps (4–8) builds maximum strength and neuromuscular power with minimal added muscle mass – added mass is pure cost for an endurance athlete. Research consistently shows heavy, low‑rep work improves running and cycling economy without the bulk of higher‑rep hypertrophy work.

Unilateral, prehab & stability work (single‑leg RDL, heel drops, planks, Pallof press): Keep at moderate‑to‑high reps (10–15, or time‑based) – these are about motor control, tendon resilience and muscular endurance. Lighter loads done well matter more than heavy loads done badly.

🔄 Practical progression through your plan:
  • Base phase (weeks 1–4, Sessions A/B): Moderate reps 10–15, lighter loads – conditions tendons and grooves movement patterns.
  • Build / Peak phases (weeks 6, 9, 11 – rotate in C/D): Heavier loads, lower reps (4–8) on big lifts, while keeping prehab exercises at their usual higher reps.
  • Recovery weeks (4, 8, 12) & Taper (13–16): Drop back to light loads and movement maintenance only – no heavy lifting in the two weeks before Tinman.
⚠️ Injury‑history notes (plantar fasciitis & calf strains):
  • Plantar fasciitis (since 2025): Keep the eccentric heel drop in every single gym session. Stop immediately if it produces sharp arch pain (mild tendon‑loading discomfort is normal, sharp pain is not).
  • Calf strains: These have historically flared when speed work sits too close to running sessions. Track/hill sessions in this plan are always followed by an easy or rest day – don't reorder the week without keeping that buffer.
  • During recovery weeks, strength load drops to mobility/activation only – this is deliberate, not something to ‘make up’.
