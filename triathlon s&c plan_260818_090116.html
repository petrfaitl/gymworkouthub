📝 EXPLANATIONS & STRATEGY

How the gym supports your swim, bike, run — and how to integrate it with your weekly schedule

🎯 Why these exercises?

🏊 Swimming

Lat pulldown / Row variations: develop lats and upper back for a strong catch and pull. The 4x6 rep range builds strength without unnecessary size.

DB overhead press: strengthens deltoids and rotator cuff for repeat overhead motion, reducing injury risk.

Farmer's carries: improve grip and forearm endurance for open-water swimming and pulling.

🚴 Cycling

Hip thrusts, RDLs, KB swings: build powerful glutes and hamstrings for the downstroke and aero position. Hip hinge patterns are crucial for time-trial efforts.

Goblet squat, Bulgarian split squat: improve force transfer through the legs without excessive spinal load.

Calf raises: maintain ankle stability during high cadence efforts.

🏃 Running

Bulgarian split squat, lunges, step-ups: single-leg strength prevents muscle imbalance and improves running economy.

RDLs & hip thrusts: hamstring and glute strength reduce overstriding and knee valgus.

Core (planks, Pallof press): maintain upright posture when fatigued, especially late in a long run.

🧘 Overall durability

High-rep accessory work (calf raises, face pulls, bird dogs) builds tendon resilience and corrects imbalances created by thousands of repetitive strokes, pedal revolutions, and strides.

🗓️ How to combine with your swim/bike/run

Tuesday (Gym + Bike): Strength session in the morning or early afternoon. Bike session later in the day or immediately after as an easy spin (20–30 min, Zone 2). Do not do high-intensity intervals after heavy leg work.

Thursday (Gym + Bike): Similar pattern. Prioritize the gym, then do an easy bike or a short tempo ride if legs feel fresh. Avoid hard bike efforts immediately following leg day.

Monday/Friday (Swim only): Use these days as recovery from gym and running. Swimming is low-impact and helps flush the legs.

Saturday (Long Ride) & Sunday (Long Run): Avoid heavy lifting the day before. Thursday is your last strength session; make sure you leave at least 48h before the long weekend efforts.

📏 Weight selection guide

You are transitioning from a 12-rep hypertrophy range to a 4x6 strength range. This means you should be able to complete all sets with excellent technique but the last two reps of each set should feel challenging. You should have about 1–2 reps in reserve (RIR = 1–2).

Your starting weights based on your current numbers:

  • Lat pulldown: Try 75 kg for 4x6. If that feels easy, move to 77–80 kg.
  • Goblet squat: Start at 32 kg for 4x6. Progress to 36 kg once you own the form.
  • Hip thrust (Hoist): Add +55 kg for 4x6–8. You may surprise yourself; many athletes can handle +65 kg with proper bracing.
  • RDL: Start with 40 kg bar for 4x8. Focus on feeling the hamstring stretch, not lower back.

🚫 Common limitations & how to avoid them

❌ Too much gym cardio: The bike and run are your cardio. Use the rower/elliptical only for 5-min warm-ups or easy recovery. Do not replace your sport-specific sessions.

❌ Soreness from leg day: If you are too sore for your swim/run, you pushed too hard. Scale back volume (3 sets instead of 4) or lower the weight slightly. Consistency beats intensity.

❌ Neglecting core: A weak core wastes energy in all three sports. Always include 2–3 core exercises per gym day, even if short.

❌ Training to failure: Avoid going to absolute failure on compound lifts. It spikes fatigue and compromises your outdoor sessions. Stop 1–2 reps short.

💡 Final tip for triathletes

Remember: the gym is supplemental. Your performance will be determined by swim, bike, and run volume and intensity. This strength plan builds the foundation that allows you to handle that training load without breaking down. When race season approaches, drop to 1 maintenance session per week (2 sets x 6–8 reps at 80% of your working weight) to keep the gains without fatigue.
