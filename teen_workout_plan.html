📋 Training Notes & Nutrition

How to progress safely, eat for bulk, and recover like a swimmer

🎯 Training Philosophy

  • Goal: build muscle safely and progressively, not test max strength.
  • Start light. Master form first. Never sacrifice technique for weight.
  • Never train to failure — always leave 2 good reps in reserve.
  • Focus on controlled lowering (eccentric) phase — 2–3 seconds down.
  • If form breaks, stop the set. Quality over quantity.

📈 Progressive Overload — How to Add Bulk

  • If he completes the top end of the rep range with perfect form: increase weight by 2.5 kg next session.
  • If adding weight is too hard, add 1–2 reps before increasing load.
  • Consistency beats intensity at this age. Two sessions per week is plenty.

🥣 Nutrition Advice

  • Calorie target: approx. 2,700–3,000 kcal/day
  • Protein: 90–110 g per day
  • Eat 3 solid meals + 1–2 snacks daily
  • Daily high-calorie smoothie:
    Milk, peanut butter, banana, oats, whey protein
  • Hydrate consistently, especially around swim sessions
💡 Bulk tip: If he’s not gaining ~0.5–0.7 kg per week after 2–3 weeks, add another snack or increase portion sizes. Swimming burns a lot of fuel.

🛌 Recovery & Injury Prevention

  • Sleep 8–10 hours per night — muscle is built during rest, not training.
  • Stretch after every session (especially lats, chest, hip flexors).
  • Use a foam roller on upper back and lats 2–3x per week.
  • Shoulder safety: Always keep shoulder blades “down and back” during pulls and presses.
  • Report any joint pain (shoulders, knees, lower back) immediately — don’t push through.
  • If soreness lasts more than 48 hours, reduce volume next session.

📆 Monthly Progress Check

  • Body weight & height
  • Key lift weights (e.g., RDL, Lat Pulldown, Leg Press)
  • Energy levels during swim training
  • Adjust calories or volume based on results
📌 Remember: At 13, connective tissue and growth plates are still developing. Slow, steady progression builds a foundation for years of athletic performance.
